THE LINKED COLONNADE. This is the territory's wonder and the one place in this
world with DELIBERATE ORDER. Read the linked-colonnade biome vocabulary
carefully; the geology rule's ban on a regular palisade does NOT apply here,
and this order must not be weathered back into an irregular cliff.

Along the east coast, the basalt columns ALL LEAN THE SAME WAY — south-east,
toward the sea — and each one touches the next at the top, joining into a
single unbroken arcade. You can walk under it. It runs the full height of the
cell from north to south along the cliff top, leaves the land at the shore, and
CONTINUES OUT TO SEA as a line of linked sea stacks, still leaning the same
way, still touching, running out past the east edge of the frame and out of the
world.

Every column the same height and the same girth as its neighbours — about 20 m
tall, 4 m across. NONE larger. NOTHING standing at the middle. The chain has no
centre and must not appear to have one. Caught mid-topple, as though one wave
passed through the rock and stayed.

The ground behind it is deliberately plain and quiet: a narrow cliff-top bench
of thin grass and bare rock running the length of the cell, nothing competing.
A few dark conifers only in the gullies at the west edge.

The COLONNADE-HEAD site, where the arcade leaves the land: the last column
standing on the bench, the first standing in the sea, and level ground beside
them wide enough to stand on and look along the line.

Surf breaks between the stacks and through the arches. The EAST edge is sea;
carry land to the NORTH, SOUTH and WEST.
